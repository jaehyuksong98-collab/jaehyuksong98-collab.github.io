Executive summary / Analysis

Start with the question the evidence can answer.

The most important design choice in the 13-week cash-flow project came before the forecast: today’s cash balance was unknown. Estimating whether a company would run out of cash would therefore require a starting balance the public information did not establish.

The model instead asks how much opening liquidity a quarter would require under explicit assumptions. That produces a scenario to inspect, while leaving the unknown balance unknown.

What the project establishes

The report presents three model outputs: NOK 26.125m for the base case, NOK 41.358m with tighter supplier terms, and NOK 58.427m when a shareholder loan is repaid. These are outputs from an independent model, not verified current cash requirements.

In the supplier scenario, changing modeled payment timing increases the cash requirement by about 58.3% without changing revenue or margin. This is a result within the model’s assumptions.

Why the distinction matters

A profitability view and a liquidity view answer different questions. Annual accounts can help calibrate a model, but they cannot identify the exact week a customer pays or a supplier invoice falls due.

For a business reader, the output’s value is partly the next set of questions it produces: what are the current bank balances, which invoices are overdue, and which payments are contractually fixed?

A connection to other sectors

As an analytical framework, the same distinction is worth exploring in shipping and real estate: an apparently attractive asset or contract does not by itself describe the timing of cash receipts and obligations.

This is a proposed research connection, not a claim that the Arbaflame model can be transferred unchanged to those sectors. Industry-specific contracts and financing structures would need separate evidence.

Risks & alternative interpretations

The supplier-days measure uses historical balance-sheet proxies. It is not a disclosed current supplier contract. A different payment schedule, cost structure, or activity level could change the result materially.

A high modeled opening requirement does not establish a funding shortfall. Existing cash, available facilities, refinancing, or changed operations could alter the real position; this external exercise does not observe all of them.

What to examine next

  • Current balances and invoice-level aging.
  • Contractual debt, payroll, tax, and supplier payment dates.
  • Scenario sensitivity to collection delays.
  • Whether updated group accounts support recalibrating the model.
