An AI watermark would not explain the work behind my analysis

OpenAI announced on October 5 that eligible ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU will receive invisible watermarks over the coming weeks. Selected API models get an optional setting globally. For me, the interesting question is how to explain my own contribution when AI helps turn financial research into readable prose.

2026-10-06BY JAEHYUK SONG

Why this caught my attention

A financial note can contain several different kinds of work: figures taken from a statement, a calculation, an assumption about the business, and a paragraph explaining the conclusion. I would not want all of those to disappear into the single description “AI-assisted.” That label leaves too much unsaid.

I am interested in using AI to make research and data processing more efficient. I can imagine asking it to tighten a paragraph after I have worked through the numbers. But I would still want a reader to understand which argument I made, what evidence supports it, and where the tool helped with expression.

What the signal would leave unanswered

OpenAI says its method places a statistical pattern in word choices. Detection can fail, particularly with short, constrained or edited text; initial detector access is restricted. The company also says a watermark does not verify accuracy or measure human contribution. These are stated limitations, not results I have independently tested.

That makes the announcement useful to think about, even without treating a detector as part of my workflow. Suppose I wrote that a company’s margins improved because pricing strengthened. A signal about who generated the sentence would not show whether I had checked the segment disclosure or confused a currency effect with an operating improvement. I would want the underlying explanation to survive scrutiny on its own.

There is a reasonable case for an origin signal: it could add context when a passage circulates without its history. My concern is the temptation to ask it a much bigger question about the quality of someone’s work. I would rather make my process understandable at the time I write than rely on a later guess about the wording.

What I would try next

For a small company-research exercise, I would keep the source extracts, my calculation sheet, an initial interpretation and the final paragraph together. If AI helped rewrite the paragraph, I would add a short note describing that role. I would then compare the two versions for changes in meaning, especially words such as “caused,” “suggests” and “will.”

I could explore using n8n to collect those files and version notes into a review folder. That would be my own proposed setup; I have not tested it or verified a watermark integration. The first useful measure would be whether I can retrace an assertion without reopening the whole research process.

Before trying the new setting, I would check the then-current model and regional eligibility rather than assume the rollout covers my account. The skill I want to build is clear authorship: knowing what I contributed and being able to show how I reached a conclusion.
