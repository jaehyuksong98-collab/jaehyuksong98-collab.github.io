Define the modeling objective and build a step-by-step approach.

Define the modeling objective

At the start of the financial modeling stage, I decide what the project needs to explain and which decision the model should support. The objective guides the work that follows.

Research methods that fit the question

I collect and compare possible approaches, then choose methods that fit the objective, industry context, and available information. I look for an effective way to answer the question rather than adding complexity for its own sake.

Structure the work in sequence

I organize the approach into practical steps: the information I need, the assumptions to make explicit, the model to build, and the outputs to examine. This gives me a clear route from the initial question to a conclusion.

Decide how to assess the result

I identify which real-world indicators could later help me evaluate the conclusion. I keep model estimates and assumptions distinct from observed results so that a later comparison is meaningful.

Why I put this stage here

A method is useful when it answers the right question. Setting the goal first gives the analysis a purpose and makes its limitations easier to explain.

A related project: an answerable cash question

In the liquidity project, the current bank balance was unknown. The model therefore asks what opening liquidity a scenario would require, rather than claiming to predict the company’s actual funding shortfall.
