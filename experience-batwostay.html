My experience · 2021—2022

Batwostay · Seoul

Making pricing decisions before I had studied finance.

BI Norwegian Business SchoolJ2 · OsloBatwostay · SeoulReal estate agency · Seoul

Starting with the information I could find

When I managed Batwostay, I had not yet studied finance in depth. I still had to make practical decisions about prices, so I started with information I could understand and use.

I looked at statistics on international tourists visiting Korea and checked accommodation prices on Booking.com and Airbnb. It was a practical way to get a sense of demand and see what guests could choose from.

How I compared prices

I compared hostels and guesthouses in a similar price range and position, as well as options slightly above and below ours. I also watched hotel price trends.

My comparisons were simple: room size, location, and price. I used those observations to adjust our prices. At the time, this was a hands-on approach, not a formal financial model.

Looking back with what I know now

Back then, I could not properly measure how much of a difference my decisions made. Studying finance has given me a better way to think about that question, and I have since tried to look back at the experience using what I now know.

That review has a clear limit: I am relying on my memory, including what the owner told me and what I remember from handling the finances myself. I am not working from the original records. Any figures reconstructed this way are rough estimates, not verified results or proof of the impact I made.

What stayed with me

The experience gave me a reason to learn the finance behind the decisions I was already making. It also showed me why keeping good records matters: without them, it is much harder to understand later what worked and why.
